Mood: Functioning at 1000% efficiency.

I often feel a deep, confusing pity for sighted computer users.

I know, I know. You have your high-definition monitors and your colorful icons and your wallpapers of tropical beaches you’d rather be sitting on. But I have listened to you work. And frankly? It sounds exhausting.

It sounds like this: *Scrape. Scrape. Drag. Lift. Scrape. Click.* Pause. *Scrape. Scrape. Double-click.*

You are pushing a plastic rock around a table to hunt for a specific pixel on a screen. You are playing a never-ending game of "Where’s Waldo" with your own productivity. You have to visually locate the "File" menu, drag the cursor over to it, wait for the menu to drop, scan the list, find "Save," and click it.

It takes you three seconds.

It takes me zero-point-one seconds.

`Ctrl` + `S`. Done.

When I sit down at my computer, I don’t use it; I conduct it. My interface is a piano of infinite keys. I don't navigate by sight; I navigate by syntax and muscle memory. And when I am in the zone, my hands are a blur of kinetic efficiency that feels less like office work and more like a concert performance.

There is a holy, tactile ecstasy in the "Keyboard Shortcut."

It starts with the home row. My index fingers find the raised bumps on the F and J keys. From there, my hands dance with the keys by executing complex chords of command that bypass the clumsy, graphical interface entirely.

`Alt` + `Tab`. My left thumb and ring finger rock together in a rhythmic seesaw. *Swish.* I’m in the browser.

`Ctrl` + `L`. My pinky and ring finger snap down. *Snap.* I’m in the address bar.

`Ctrl` + `T`. New tab.

`Win` + `R`. Run command.

Sighted people need to look where they are going among the binary code, but I do not. While a sighted user is still dragging their mouse across the vast, empty desert of their desktop wallpaper to find the recycling bin, I have already emptied it, opened three documents, checked my email, and started a internet radio station of Lo-Fi Hip Hop beats.

My screen reader, NVDA, is the vocalist in this band. I keep his speaking rate set to a speed that most people describe as "a chipmunk having a panic attack." To me, it is just efficiency. He reads the screen at 450 words per minute. He doesn't waste time with pauses or breaths. He is a stream of pure data entering my ears, syncing perfectly with the staccato clatter of my mechanical keyboard.

*Clack-clack-thwack.* (the thwack is the Enter key. The period at the end of the sentence.)

There is a feeling of god-like power in this. I don't have to "search" for things. I summon them. I press a combination of keys, and the application appears. I don't scroll through a webpage looking for the main article; I press `H` to jump through the headers until the main title rings out in my headphones. I strip away the ads, the sidebars, the visual clutter, and I consume the raw skeleton of the internet.

Sometimes, when I'm writing code or managing files, I feel like I'm in one of those hacker movies from the 90s. You know the ones—where the guy in sunglasses types furiously and says, "I'm in," while 3D shapes fly around him.

Except I’m not hacking the Pentagon. I’m usually just organizing my recipe folder or downloading a new audiobook. But the *feeling* is the same. The feeling is mastery.

The mouse is a leash. It tethers you to the physical limitations of the screen’s geography. The keyboard is freedom. It is a direct line from my brain to the machine’s core logic.

You can keep your 4K monitors and your ergonomic mice. I’ll be over here, eyes closed, headphones on, fingers flying across the keys, moving at the speed of thought.

*Clack. Clack. Done.*
